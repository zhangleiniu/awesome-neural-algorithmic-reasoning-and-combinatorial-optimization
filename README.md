# Awesome Neural Algorithmic Reasoning and Combinatorial Optimization

[![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

A curated list of papers on **neural algorithmic reasoning** (NAR) and **machine learning for combinatorial optimization** (ML4CO) — end-to-end neural solvers, learned components inside MILP and SAT solvers, expressivity and learnability theory, benchmarks, and the replication debates.

These are usually treated as two literatures. They are listed together here because they share one commitment: **a computational task that a classical algorithm was previously performing is instead performed, wholly or in part, by a learned model.** That covers the network that emits the answer outright, the network judged against an algorithm's execution trace, the network replacing one decision inside an algorithm that otherwise runs unchanged, and the network replacing a subroutine the algorithm calls. It also covers the theory about whether any of that is possible, and the empirical work re-examining whether a reported instance of it held up.

Two things here are not in other lists. **[Debates and Replications](#debates-and-replications)** lays out the published exchanges in order, including the replies — several widely repeated "refutations" are contested and are marked as such. **[What the Theory Actually Proves](#what-the-theory-actually-proves)** separates expressivity from learnability from generalisation, and records the assumption each result rests on.

## Debates and Replications

Claims in this field get contested more often than the citation graph suggests, and the threads rarely join up. Each row below is a published exchange, in order. **A comment is not a refutation** — where a reply exists it is listed, and where the disagreement is still open this list says so rather than picking a winner.

### Physics-inspired GNNs for maximum independent set and MaxCut

| # | Paper | Position |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Schuetz, Brubaker & Katzgraber, *Combinatorial optimization with physics-inspired graph neural networks*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-022-00468-6), **Nat. Mach. Intell. 2022** | Relaxes the QUBO Hamiltonian into a differentiable loss, trains a GNN unsupervised, projects back to integers; claims scaling to millions of variables. |
| 2 | [Angelini & Ricci-Tersenghi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-022-00589-y), **Nat. Mach. Intell. 2022** | A near-linear-time greedy algorithm finds better MIS solutions, faster. Proposes a harder benchmark. |
| 3 | [Boettcher](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-022-00587-0), **Nat. Mach. Intell. 2022** | On MaxCut over sparse *d*-regular random graphs the results are "only minutely better than those for gradient descent and are outperformed by greedy algorithms". |
| 4 | [Schuetz, Brubaker & Katzgraber, *Reply to: Inability of a graph neural network heuristic…*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-022-00588-z), **Nat. Mach. Intell. 2022** | Contests the instance distribution the comments chose, with new numbers. |
| 5 | [Gamarnik, *Barriers for the performance of GNNs in discrete random structures*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2314092120), **PNAS 2023** | An architecture-independent barrier from the overlap gap property — but see the note below on which problem it covers. |

**What this thread does and does not settle.** The reply at step 4 contests the instance distribution, so the thread is a live disagreement, not a closed refutation. And the barrier at step 5 does not cover the experiment at step 3: its independent-set theorem is about random *d*-regular graphs, which is Angelini's target, while its MaxCut theorem needs *K*-uniform hypergraphs with *K* ≥ 4 even. For *K* = 2 — Boettcher's actual experiment — the paper itself says the overlap gap property conjecturally does not hold and GNN optimality "might be achievable". The barrier also does not extend to dense instances, and cannot: R-locality constrains nothing once the R-hop neighbourhood is the whole graph.

**Known gap in this list.** Schuetz et al. published a second reply, to Angelini & Ricci-Tersenghi (*Nat. Mach. Intell.* 5, 32–34, 2023). It is not yet included.

### The ablation that was found four times

Four sub-communities independently ablated the learned component and found the non-learned scaffold carried most of the reported gain. They use four vocabularies, target four method families, and — with one exception — do not cite each other.

| Paper | Target | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| [Böther et al.](https://openreview.net/forum?id=mk0HzdqY7i1), **ICLR 2022** | Li et al.'s guided tree search for MIS | "replacing the GNN output with random values performs similar to using [the learned values]"; the work is done by graph kernelization. |
| [Ma et al., *ML4CO-Bench-101*](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/aa9340420e8d78021c35ae984e1bda85-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html), **NeurIPS 2025** | Neural CO solvers generally | Post-processing "often mask[s] raw model performance … possibly skewing their conclusions". Names it "post-processing abuse". |
| [Aggarwal et al.](https://openreview.net/forum?id=mX0tP6kivu), **LoG 2025** | Neural algorithmic reasoning on CLRS | "a simple GNN outperforms NAR in 11/24 (45.8%) of the tasks we tried". Traces it to CLRS test sets fixing instance size rather than sampling a size distribution. |
| [Cheng & Basu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23249), **ICML 2026** | Learned branch-and-cut | The theoretical form: local score-based methods can produce search trees exponentially larger than optimal, because the expert signal is misaligned with tree size. |

The one paper that cites across these threads is [*FrontierCO*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16952) (ICLR 2026).

## What the Theory Actually Proves

Expressivity, learnability and generalisation are three different questions and the literature routinely conflates them. This table separates them for the load-bearing results, and records the assumption each one rests on — because in almost every case that assumption is where the result's practical reach ends.

| Result | About which representation | Kind | Rests on |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Sato et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10261), **NeurIPS 2019** | plain graph, varying node features | possibility **and** impossibility, tight | Equivalence with distributed local algorithms. Maximum matching moves from *no constant-factor approximation* to *any α > 1* by adding a 2-colouring alone. |
| [Loukas](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03199), **ICLR 2020** | plain graph | impossibility | Depth–width product; results imported from distributed computing. |
| [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10759), **ICLR 2023** (MILP) | variable–constraint bipartite graph | impossibility, with a characterised failing class | WL separation power. Repaired by appending random features, or by restricting to *unfoldable* MILPs. |
| [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.12288), **ICLR 2023** (LP) | variable–constraint bipartite graph | possibility | The same WL connection, favourable verdict. |
| [Chen et al.](https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-3939), **NeurIPS 2024** | bipartite graph, strong-branching scores | possibility on a named class | The *MP-tractable* class, defined relative to that encoding; outside it, second-order GNNs are needed. |
| [Qian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27786), **ICML 2026** (SDP) | bipartite graph | impossibility | Standard bipartite representations fail to capture equivariance under simultaneous row and column permutation; repaired by a 2-FWL-equivalent architecture. |
| [Yau et al.](https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-2328), **NeurIPS 2024** | Max-CSP constraint graph | possibility | Polynomial-time optimality **assuming the Unique Games Conjecture**; the absorbed object is the SDP relaxation, and the bound on the optimum comes from the relaxation rather than the network. |
| [Hertrich & Skutella](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v35i9.16939), **AAAI 2021** | Knapsack as a ReLU RNN over items | possibility, with matching lower bounds | Depth four, **width quadratic in the profit of the optimal solution** — the construction is the pseudo-polynomial DP. |
| [Gamarnik](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2314092120), **PNAS 2023** | any GNN on random discrete structures | impossibility | Overlap gap property and R-locality. Independent set on random *d*-regular graphs; the MaxCut theorem needs *K*-uniform hypergraphs, *K* ≥ 4 even. Does not extend to dense instances. |
| [Wittig et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13106), **ICML 2026** | problem → DP state graph → shortest path | possibility | Sufficient conditions for an MPNN to learn an algorithm from small instances; covers SSSP, MST and 0-1 knapsack. See the qualifier below. |
| [Nerem et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/nerem26a.html), **COLT 2026** | shortest-path instances | possibility | A GNN minimising a sparsity-regularised loss implements *K* steps of Bellman-Ford. See the qualifier below. |

**A qualifier that applies to every positive learnability result here.** Read at theorem level, the strongest positive results on the polynomial side are *parameter-identification* arguments, not generalisation arguments. They require an aligned architecture, an explicitly constructed witness training set, and a loss driven within ε of its **global** minimum; Wittig et al. additionally require a regularization parameter exponential in the step count, and state that sampling-based construction of informative training sets "remains an important direction for future work". No paper in this list establishes that training reaches the minimum its guarantee conditions on.

**And a pattern across the positive results.** No result here shows a network absorbing an NP-hard task as such. Each one either absorbs a polynomial object — an SDP or LP relaxation, a DP recurrence, a dual ascent — or holds under a condition that can be dropped: the Unique Games Conjecture, MP-tractability, unfoldability, bounded profit. This is an observation about the literature as it stands, not a theorem about what is possible.

## Neural Algorithmic Reasoning

Networks trained to execute an algorithm — judged against its intermediate steps, not only its output. The field standardised on one encoding in 2022 and has argued about everything else since.

### Blueprint and Core Architectures

The encoder–processor–decoder framing and the architectures that established it.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Patterns'21</i>) Neural algorithmic reasoning</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2021.100273">link</a>]</summary> The opinion piece that named the field: build networks that execute algorithmic computation, and run classical algorithms on inputs previously inaccessible to them.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'22</i>) A Generalist Neural Algorithmic Learner</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v198/ibarz22a.html">link</a>]</summary> One GNN processor executing all thirty CLRS algorithms, via a series of improvements to input representation, training regime and processor architecture; multi-task performance approaching single-task.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'20</i>) Neural Execution of Graph Algorithms</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=SkgKO0EtvS">link</a>]</summary> Trains GNNs to imitate the individual <i>steps</i> of classical graph algorithms — BFS, Bellman-Ford, Prim — rather than to recover a final answer. Finds max-aggregation best suited, and shows learning reachability jointly substantially improves shortest paths.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'20</i>) Pointer Graph Networks</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/176bf6219855a6eb1f3a30903e34b6fb-Abstract.html">link</a>]</summary> Augments sets or graphs with dynamically inferred pointer edges, supervised directly with operation sequences over disjoint-set union and link/cut trees; extrapolates to 5× larger inputs.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'20</i>) Neural Execution Engines: Learning to Execute Subroutines</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/c8b9abffb45bf79a630fb613dcd23449-Abstract.html">link</a>]</summary> Studies generalisation at the level of numerical subroutines; finds transformer seq2seq models learn to sort but degrade rapidly beyond training lengths, traces this to attention weights losing fidelity on numerically similar inputs, and proposes learned conditional masking.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'16</i>) Neural GPUs Learn Algorithms</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08228">link</a>]</summary> A computationally universal, highly parallel architecture built on convolutional gated recurrent units; trained on short instances of an algorithmic task and generalises to much longer ones.</details>

### Generalisation, Training and Supervision

Work that keeps the CLRS encoding fixed and changes what the architecture maintains, what it is supervised on, or how it aggregates.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'26</i>) Richer Representations for Neural Algorithmic Reasoning via Auxiliary Reconstruction</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.48448/ejt3-fp46">link</a>]</summary> Attacks the <i>encoder</i> of the encoder-processor-decoder stack with an auxiliary state-reconstruction objective, so intermediate states are represented more richly.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'25</i>) Discrete Neural Algorithmic Reasoning</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11628">link</a>]</summary> Forces the execution trajectory to be a combination of finitely many predefined discrete states, separating discrete from continuous data flow; supervised on state transitions, the models "perfectly align with the original algorithm" and correctness can be proven on any test data.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'24</i>) On the Markov Property of Neural Algorithmic Reasoning: Analyses and Methods</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04929">link</a>]</summary> Removes historical embeddings because they contradict the Markov nature of algorithm execution, then reintroduces them behind a gate to ease early training. Better generalization on CLRS-30.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) Deep Equilibrium Algorithmic Reasoning</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-1059">link</a>]</summary> Since an algorithm's solution is often an equilibrium, solve the equilibrium equation directly rather than iterating step by step. Needs no ground-truth step count at train or test time.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) Open-Book Neural Algorithmic Reasoning</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00072">link</a>]</summary> Lets the network access and use all training instances while reasoning about a given one, at train and test time; attention-based aggregation across tasks also exposes relationships between CLRS algorithms.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'24</i>) Recurrent Aggregators in Neural Algorithmic Reasoning</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07154">link</a>]</summary> Replaces the permutation-equivariant aggregator with an RNN. State of the art on Heapsort and Quickselect.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'23</i>) Neural Algorithmic Reasoning with Causal Regularisation</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10258">link</a>]</summary> Observes that many different inputs induce identical intermediate computations, builds data augmentations from that, and enforces next-step invariance across them via a self-supervised objective formalised in a causal graph. Up to 3× improvement on CLRS OOD.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'23</i>) Asynchronous Algorithmic Alignment with Cocycles</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v231/dudzik24a.html">link</a>]</summary> Explicitly separates node state update from message function invocation, yielding a formulation that can reason about asynchronous computation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'23</i>) Parallel Algorithms Align with Neural Execution</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v231/engelmayer24a.html">link</a>]</summary> Argues neural reasoners are parallel processors, so teaching them sequential algorithms wastes computation; parallel implementations of searching, sorting and SCC train far faster and often predict better.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'23</i>) Recursive Algorithmic Reasoning</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v231/jurss24a.html">link</a>]</summary> Augments GNNs with a stack and improves trajectory sampling for alignment with recursive algorithms; substantially better generalisation on DFS.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'23</i>) Dual Algorithmic Reasoning</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04496">link</a>]</summary> Exploits duality of the underlying optimisation problem by learning both algorithms simultaneously (max-flow / min-cut); validated on brain vessel classification.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'23</i>) Neural Algorithmic Reasoning Without Intermediate Supervision</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-2249">link</a>]</summary> Learns from input–output pairs only, no algorithm trajectory, using architectural improvements plus a self-supervised objective regularising intermediate computation. Competitive with trajectory-supervised counterparts; state of the art on sorting.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'22</i>) Graph Neural Networks are Dynamic Programmers</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15544">link</a>]</summary> Uses category theory and abstract algebra to make the GNN–DP correspondence precise, beyond case-by-case observations like Bellman-Ford, and derives better-grounded architectures for edge-centric tasks.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'22</i>) Learnable Commutative Monoids for Graph Neural Networks</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v198/ong22a.html">link</a>]</summary> Shows recurrent aggregators beat permutation-invariant ones on set-aggregation problems that do not generalise to unbounded inputs, then removes their O(V) depth cost by learning commutative associative binary operators, giving an O(log V) aggregator.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'20</i>) Towards Scale-Invariant Graph-related Problem Solving by Iterative Homogeneous GNNs</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/b64a70760bb75e3ecfd1ad86d8f10c88-Abstract.html">link</a>]</summary> Learns to terminate message passing adaptively according to computation progress, and introduces homogeneous transformation layers that are universal homogeneous function approximators.</details>

### What the Models Learn

Studies of the representation a trained reasoner induces, as opposed to the one it is given.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'26</i>) Intrinsic Task Symmetry Drives Generalization in Algorithmic Tasks</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.01968">link</a>]</summary> Argues intrinsic task symmetries drive grokking, inducing structured low-dimensional geometry; identifies a three-stage dynamic (memorisation, symmetry acquisition, geometric organisation) and locates generalisation onset in the second.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'23</i>) Latent Space Representations of Neural Algorithmic Reasoners</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v231/mirjanic24a.html">link</a>]</summary> Analyses the latent space a GNN induces while executing algorithms, identifies loss of resolution and inability to handle out-of-range values, and fixes both with a softmax aggregator and latent space decay.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'22</i>) Algorithmic Concept-Based Explainable Reasoning</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v36i6.20623">link</a>]</summary> Applies concept-based explanation to GNNs executing algorithms; concept-bottleneck GNNs extract propositional formulas over learned concepts while matching state-of-the-art performance.</details>

### Toward NP-hard Problems

Attempts to carry the blueprint past the polynomial-time algorithms it was built on.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'25</i>) Tropical Attention: Neural Algorithmic Reasoning for Combinatorial Algorithms</b> [<a href="https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/8e0be7875ffe7dcfea07e35c79e1a923-Abstract-Conference.html">link</a>]</summary> Lifts the attention kernel into tropical projective space so reasoning is piecewise-linear and 1-Lipschitz, preserving polyhedral decision structure; proves multi-head tropical attention stacks universally approximate tropical circuits and realize tropical transitive closure, "achieving <b>polynomial resource bounds</b> without invoking recurrent mechanisms".</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'25</i>) Primal-Dual Neural Algorithmic Reasoning</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24067">link</a>]</summary> A NAR framework on the primal-dual paradigm, aligning GNNs with primal-dual algorithms via a bipartite primal/dual representation, using optimal solutions from small instances; outperforms the approximation algorithms it simulates and integrates with commercial solvers.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'25</i>) KNARsack: Teaching Neural Algorithmic Reasoners to Solve Pseudo-Polynomial Problems</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15239">link</a>]</summary> A neural reasoner for Knapsack following the two-phase pipeline: build the DP table, then reconstruct the solution from it, with DP supervision on intermediate states. Generalises better than direct prediction.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'25</i>) Tackling GNARLy Problems: Graph Neural Algorithmic Reasoning Reimagined through Reinforcement Learning</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18930">link</a>]</summary> Reframes learning algorithm trajectories as an MDP, unlocking imitation and RL; addresses solution validity, multiple correct solutions, and problems with no known expert algorithm.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'23</i>) Neural Algorithmic Reasoning for Combinatorial Optimisation</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v231/georgiev24a.html">link</a>]</summary> Pre-trains the neural model on relevant classical algorithms before training on CO instances; beats models with no algorithmic prior.</details>

### Transformers and LLMs as Executors

Sequence models asked to execute algorithms directly, and hybrids that attach a reasoner to a language model.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'24</i>) Transformers meet Neural Algorithmic Reasoners</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09308">link</a>]</summary> Hybrid architecture where language-model tokens cross-attend to the node embeddings of a GNN-based NAR, trained in two phases; gains over Transformer-only models on CLRS-Text in and out of distribution.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) Transformers Can Do Arithmetic with the Right Embeddings</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17399">link</a>]</summary> Adds an embedding encoding each digit's position relative to the start of the number; training on 20-digit numbers reaches 99% accuracy on 100-digit addition.</details>

### Applications and Transfer

The blueprint applied outside its benchmark, and transfer between algorithms or modalities.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'25</i>) Leveraging Classical Algorithms for Graph Neural Networks</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.21574">link</a>]</summary> Pre-trains GNNs on 24 CLRS algorithms, then freezes selected layers for molecular property prediction; Segments Intersect pre-training yields 6% absolute gain on ogbg-molhiv.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'24</i>) NAR-*ICP: Neural Execution of Classical ICP-based Pointcloud Registration Algorithms</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11031">link</a>]</summary> Learns the intermediate computations of classical ICP-based point-cloud registration, extending the CLRS benchmark; outperforms baselines and the algorithms it was trained on.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'22</i>) Learning to Configure Computer Networks with Neural Algorithmic Reasoning</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01980">link</a>]</summary> Relaxes the hard search for a configuration satisfying a specification into an approximate objective, then trains a neural algorithmic model to generate configurations likely to satisfy it. Up to 490× faster than SMT-based synthesis, satisfying &gt;92% of requirements on average.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'22</i>) Continuous Neural Algorithmic Planners</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v198/he22a.html">link</a>]</summary> Extends XLVIN from discrete to continuous action spaces by discretisation, with selective expansion policies for large planning graphs; gains in MuJoCo, especially low-data.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'21</i>) Neural Algorithmic Reasoners are Implicit Planners</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05442">link</a>]</summary> Identifies an algorithmic bottleneck in value-iteration-style implicit planners — running the planner on scalar predictions at every state hurts data efficiency when those scalars are poorly estimated — and instead executes value iteration in a high-dimensional latent space.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'22</i>) Reasoning-Modulated Representations</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v198/velickovic22a.html">link</a>]</summary> Incorporates known structure about the data-generating system into a pre-trained reasoning module and studies how it shapes representations in self-supervised learning from pixels.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'21</i>) How to transfer algorithmic reasoning knowledge to learn new algorithms?</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14056">link</a>]</summary> Studies how reasoning knowledge learned from algorithms with execution traces transfers to tasks with only input–output examples; finds standard transfer techniques insufficient and multi-task learning effective.</details>

## End-to-End Neural Solvers

The network emits a solution and no classical algorithm remains to certify it. What degrades when the prediction is wrong is correctness, which is why this is the part of the field the critiques below concentrate on.

### Construction

The network builds a solution, autoregressively or in one shot.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'25</i>) COExpander: Adaptive Solution Expansion for Combinatorial Optimization</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/ma25r.html">link</a>]</summary> Diagnoses two scaling bottlenecks and proposes Adaptive Expansion: a globally predicted heatmap guided by locally determined partial solutions directs expansion.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'25</i>) UniCO: On Unified Combinatorial Optimization via Problem Reduction to Matrix-Encoded General TSP</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=yEwakMNIex">link</a>]</summary> Unifies a set of CO problems by reducing them to a general TSP form encoded as distance matrices, so one neural solver trains across problem types.</details>

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'25</i>) BIPNN: Learning to Solve Binary Integer Programming via Hypergraph Neural Networks</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20997">link</a>]</summary> Reformulates constrained, discrete, nonlinear binary integer programs into unconstrained differentiable polynomial losses optimized by a hypergraph neural network, unsupervised.</details>

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'24</i>) Effectiveness of SDP Rounding Using Hopfield Networks</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v269/curran25a.html">link</a>]</summary> Uses Hopfield networks to perform the <i>rounding</i> step of an SDP relaxation — the standard template of approximation-algorithm design — on Max-Cut, Max-Clique and graph colouring.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'24</i>) Towards a General Recipe for Combinatorial Optimization with Multi-Filter GNNs</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v269/wenkel25a.html">link</a>]</summary> A GNN with a complex filter bank and localized attention for CO on graphs, trained unsupervised; competitive on max cut, minimum dominating set and maximum clique, on par with Gurobi on max-cut.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'22</i>) Neural Set Function Extensions: Learning with Discrete Functions in High Dimensions</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04055">link</a>]</summary> A framework extending set functions onto low-dimensional continuous domains, subsuming many known extensions, then lifting them into high-dimensional representations "taking inspiration from the success of semidefinite programs".</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'22</i>) DIMES: A Differentiable Meta Solver for Combinatorial Optimization Problems</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04123">link</a>]</summary> Parameterizes the distribution over candidate solutions in a compact continuous space, avoiding autoregressive decoding, with a meta-learning initialization. - <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Nat. Mach. Intell.'22</i>) Combinatorial optimization with physics-inspired graph neural networks</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-022-00468-6">link</a>]</summary> Relaxes the QUBO Hamiltonian into a differentiable loss, trains a GNN unsupervised, projects back to integers; claims scaling to millions of variables.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Phys. Rev. Research'22</i>) Graph coloring with physics-inspired graph neural networks</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.4.043131">link</a>]</summary> PI-GNN applied to graph colouring as multiclass node classification, unsupervised via the Potts model; includes a real scheduling application.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'22</i>) Unsupervised Learning for Combinatorial Optimization with Principled Objective Relaxation</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05984">link</a>]</summary> Relaxation-plus-rounding with a network parameterizing the relaxed solution, trained end-to-end. - <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'20</i>) Erdos Goes Neural: an Unsupervised Learning Framework for Combinatorial Optimization on Graphs</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10643">link</a>]</summary> Trains a GNN unsupervised with a loss whose "minima are guaranteed to be low-cost valid solutions", giving "a probabilistic certificate for the existence of a low cost feasible solution", then extracts a discrete solution "by the method of conditional expectation".</details>

### Diffusion and Sampling

Generative models over the solution space.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'26</i>) Unsupervised Diffusion Solver for Combinatorial Optimization via Combinatorial Adjoint Matching</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30920">link</a>]</summary> Formulates diffusion-based CO as stochastic control over continuous-time Markov chains, with discrete adjoint dynamics giving structured low-variance trajectory-level training signals; unsupervised.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) Fast T2T: Optimization Consistency Speeds Up Diffusion-Based Training-to-Testing Solving for Combinatorial Optimization</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02941">link</a>]</summary> Learns direct mappings from any noise level to the optimal solution via an optimization-consistency protocol, enabling single-step generation.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'24</i>) A Diffusion Model Framework for Unsupervised Neural Combinatorial Optimization</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01661">link</a>]</summary> A loss upper-bounding the reverse KL divergence removes the exact-likelihood requirement, admitting diffusion models; new state of the art on data-free CO.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'23</i>) DIFUSCO: Graph-based Diffusion Solvers for Combinatorial Optimization</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08224">link</a>]</summary> Formulates NP-complete problems in discrete {0,1} space and generates solutions by graph-based denoising diffusion; compares Gaussian against Bernoulli noise.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'23</i>) Variational Annealing on Graphs for Combinatorial Optimization</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-2792">link</a>]</summary> Shows the statistical-independence assumption behind most probabilistic unsupervised CO caps performance on hard instances; an autoregressive model capturing dependencies does better. ### Training Objectives and Policy Learning

Papers whose contribution is the loss, the preference signal or the search policy rather than the encoding.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'26</i>) UCPO: A Universal Constrained Combinatorial Optimization Method via Preference Optimization</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i43.41017">link</a>]</summary> A plug-and-play preference-based loss embedding constraint satisfaction, requiring no architectural change to the host solver.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'25</i>) BOPO: Neural Combinatorial Optimization via Best-anchored and Objective-guided Preference Optimization</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07580">link</a>]</summary> Replaces sparse-reward RL with preference optimization over solutions ranked by objective value, with best-anchored pairs and an objective-guided pairwise loss; no reward model or reference policy.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'25</i>) Regularized Langevin Dynamics for Combinatorial Optimization</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00277">link</a>]</summary> Enforces an expected distance between sampled and current solutions to escape local minima; instantiated as both a simulated-annealing and a neural solver.</details>

## Learning Inside Classical Solvers

One decision inside an exact solver is replaced and the rest runs unchanged, so a wrong prediction costs time rather than correctness — the solver still proves its answer. The MILP encoding argument running through these sections, from hand-crafted features in 2016 to an impossibility theorem in 2023, is the longest sustained representation debate in either community.

### Branching

Variable selection in branch-and-bound — the longest-running representation argument in the field.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'26</i>) Planning in Branch-and-Bound: Model-Based Reinforcement Learning for Exact Combinatorial Optimization</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i30.39759">link</a>]</summary> Model-based RL that plans over a learned internal model of B&amp;B dynamics to select branching variables — the simulator-plus-MCTS recipe from board games.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'26</i>) Generative Branching for Mixed-Integer Linear Programming</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i17.38450">link</a>]</summary> Recasts branching variable selection as conditional generation: a diffusion model produces diverse branching scores, distilled by consistency modelling into one-step inference conditioned on the B&amp;B state.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'25</i>) SORREL: Suboptimal-Demonstration-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Learning to Branch</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i11.33219">link</a>]</summary> Learns to branch from <i>suboptimal</i> demonstrations selected by value estimation, combining offline and online RL.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'24</i>) Towards Imitation Learning to Branch for MIP: A Hybrid Reinforcement Learning based Sample Augmentation Approach</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=NdcQQ82mfy">link</a>]</summary> An online RL agent decides, per state, whether to draw the training sample from the learned policy or the expert, augmenting imitation data cost-effectively.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'22</i>) Learning to Branch with Tree MDPs</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11107">link</a>]</summary> Learns branching from scratch with RL, introducing tree MDPs — "a generalization of temporal MDPs that provides a more suitable framework for learning to branch" — and a tree policy gradient theorem with better credit assignment.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS Workshop'21</i>) Improving Learning to Branch via Reinforcement Learning</b> [<a href="https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4b6b99a9ce7120aac9aadcab7d60843e58d5f09">link</a>]</summary> Argues strong branching is a poor imitation target once its side effects are removed, and trains non-myopic branching policies with RL plus a novelty-search evolutionary strategy.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'21</i>) Parameterizing Branch-and-Bound Search Trees to Learn Branching Policies</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v35i5.16512">link</a>]</summary> Argues for branching policies that generalise across heterogeneous MILPs by parameterizing the state of the search tree itself, not only the current node.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'20</i>) Hybrid Models for Learning to Branch</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15212">link</a>]</summary> Asks whether the GNN branching model is competitive when only a CPU is available (answer: no), and proposes a GNN/MLP hybrid that retains predictive power at much lower cost.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'20</i>) Solving Mixed Integer Programs Using Neural Networks</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13349">link</a>]</summary> Neural Diving produces high-quality partial assignments; Neural Branching imitates a GPU-scalable variant of full strong branching. Large gains over SCIP on MIPLIB and Google production data.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'19</i>) Exact Combinatorial Optimization with Graph Convolutional Neural Networks</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01629">link</a>]</summary> Encodes the MILP as its natural bipartite graph and learns branching by imitating strong branching. First learned policy to beat a solver's own rules on large problems.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'16</i>) Learning to Branch in Mixed Integer Programming</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v30i1.10080">link</a>]</summary> Learns a cheap surrogate for strong branching, framing variable selection as learning-to-rank and training on the fly while solving one instance.</details>

### Cutting Planes

Which cuts to add, how many, and in what order.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'26</i>) MIRACLE: Model-free Imitation and Reinforcement Learning for Adaptive Cut-Selection</b> [<a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=zZyxHmId3w">link</a>]</summary> A PPO-trained behavioural model imitating the expert solver drives a cut-selection policy with curriculum learning and adaptive inference budgets; 98.1% memory reduction at ~0.08% objective gap.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'23</i>) Learning Cut Selection for Mixed-Integer Linear Programming via Hierarchical Sequence Model</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00244">link</a>]</summary> First method to handle which cuts, how many, and in what order simultaneously: a two-level model where the upper level sets the count and the lower does seq2seq selection, trained with RL.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'22</i>) Learning to Cut by Looking Ahead: Cutting Plane Selection via Imitation Learning</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13414">link</a>]</summary> Shows a greedy lookahead rule selecting cuts by best bound improvement is strong but too expensive, then trains a network to imitate that lookahead expert.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'20</i>) Reinforcement Learning for Integer Programming: Learning to Cut</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04859">link</a>]</summary> Casts cutting-plane selection as RL with a deep formulation, architecture and algorithms; beats human-designed heuristics and generalises to larger instances and other problem classes.</details>

### Node Selection, Diving and Primal Heuristics

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'23</i>) Learning To Dive In Branch And Bound</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09943">link</a>]</summary> Learns diving heuristics with GNNs, training generative models to predict variable assignments and using LP duality to turn predictions into diving decisions. - <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'22</i>) MIP-GNN: A Data-Driven Framework for Guiding Combinatorial Solvers</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v36i9.21262">link</a>]</summary> A GNN over the bipartite graph predicts variable biases; one trained model then guides node selection and warm-starting.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'21</i>) Learning to Schedule Heuristics in Branch and Bound</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10294">link</a>]</summary> The first data-driven framework for scheduling which primal heuristics to run and when inside an exact MIP solver.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'14</i>) Learning to Search in Branch and Bound Algorithms</b> [<a href="https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2014/hash/533d190f5aa2926b2a8a30c8bea0e05d-Abstract.html">link</a>]</summary> Learns an adaptive node-searching order for any problem class solvable by B&amp;B, via imitation learning; beaten against SCIP and Gurobi on four MIP libraries.</details>

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>arXiv'22</i>) Learning a Large Neighborhood Search Algorithm for Mixed Integer Programs</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10201">link</a>]</summary> Learned LNS for MIPs in two parts: Neural Diving produces the initial assignment, then the search is cast as an MDP and a Neural Neighborhood Selection policy picks the neighborhood at each step, searched by a MIP solver. - <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'21</i>) Learning Large Neighborhood Search Policy for Integer Programming</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03466">link</a>]</summary> Deep RL learns the LNS <i>destroy</i> operator, selecting which variables to re-optimize; an IP solver repairs. ### Learned Heuristics for Graph Search

Learned admissible-ish heuristics dropped into A* and guided local search.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>LoG'22</i>) Learning Graph Search Heuristics</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v198/pandy22a.html">link</a>]</summary> Learns a GNN search heuristic from search trajectories and ground-truth shortest-path distances, running in constant time independent of graph size and dropping into A*. Explores 58.5% fewer nodes on average.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'22</i>) Graph Neural Network Guided Local Search for the Traveling Salesperson Problem</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05291">link</a>]</summary> A GNN predicts the <b>regret</b> of including each edge; Guided Local Search consumes those predictions. Mean TSP-100 optimality gap 1.534% → 0.705%.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>CVPR'21</i>) Combinatorial Learning of Graph Edit Distance via Dynamic Embedding</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr46437.2021.00520">link</a>]</summary> DP-inspired dynamic reuse of node embeddings as a learned heuristic inside A*, pruning suboptimal branches; first deep GED method that recovers the edit path.</details>

### Algorithm Configuration and Selection

Choosing or tuning the solver rather than any decision inside it.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'25</i>) $textttSTRCMP$: Integrating Graph Structural Priors with Language Models for Combinatorial Optimization</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11057">link</a>]</summary> A GNN extracts structural embeddings from CO instances and conditions an LLM to emit solver-specific code, refined by an evolutionary process.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>IJCAI'11</i>) Hydra-MIP: Automated Algorithm Configuration and Selection for Mixed Integer Programming</b> [<a href="https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/302f936148bb19527646b523456d36ff7afbd505">link</a>]</summary> Derives a portfolio of solver configurations via automated configuration, then selects among them per instance, with a classification-based selection using a non-uniform loss.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>CPAIOR'10</i>) Automated Configuration of Mixed Integer Programming Solvers</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-13520-0_23">link</a>]</summary> Applies automated algorithm configuration to CPLEX, GUROBI and LPSOLVE, substantially improving all three.</details>

## Theory

See also [What the Theory Actually Proves](#what-the-theory-actually-proves), which separates these results by what kind of question they answer and what each one assumes.

### Expressivity and Representability

What a given encoding and architecture can represent at all.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'26</i>) On the Expressive Power of GNNs to Solve Linear SDPs</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27786">link</a>]</summary> Negative results showing standard GNN architectures fail to recover linear SDP solutions; identifies a more expressive architecture that captures SDP structure and emulates a first-order solver. Warm-starting gives up to 80% speedups.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'25</i>) Expressive Power of Graph Neural Networks for (Mixed-Integer) Quadratic Programs</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05938">link</a>]</summary> Proves MPNNs universally represent feasibility, optimal value and optimal solutions for convex QPs; in the mixed-integer setting they are not universal approximators, and a more expressive construction is identified.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'25</i>) Positional Attention: Expressivity and Learnability of Algorithmic Computation</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01686">link</a>]</summary> Transformers whose attention weights depend only on positional encodings retain the expressivity of parallel computational models at a logarithmic depth cost; analyses in-distribution learnability and the effect of parameter norms on sample complexity.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) Are Graph Neural Networks Optimal Approximation Algorithms?</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-2328">link</a>]</summary> Proves polynomial-sized message-passing GNNs "can learn the most powerful <b>polynomial time</b> algorithms for Max Constraint Satisfaction Problems assuming the Unique Games Conjecture", and builds OptGNN from it; the capture of convex relaxations also yields bounds on the optimum.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) On the Power of Small-size Graph Neural Networks for Linear Programming</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-1222">link</a>]</summary> Proves polylogarithmic-depth, constant-width GNNs suffice for packing and covering LPs, by simulating a gradient-descent variant on a potential function. Introduces GD-Net.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'24</i>) Simulation of Graph Algorithms with Looped Transformers</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01107">link</a>]</summary> Proves by construction that a looped transformer with graph-interacting attention heads simulates Dijkstra, BFS, DFS and Kosaraju, with parameter count independent of graph size; shows a finite-precision limit and constant-width Turing completeness.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AISTATS'24</i>) Exploring the Power of Graph Neural Networks in Solving Linear Optimization Problems</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10603">link</a>]</summary> Shows MPNNs can simulate standard interior-point methods for LPs, explaining their success at imitating heuristics like strong branching, and can serve as a lightweight LP proxy.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) Understanding Transformer Reasoning Capabilities via Graph Algorithms</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18512">link</a>]</summary> A representational hierarchy separating nine algorithmic reasoning problems by the parameter scaling regime that solves them; proves logarithmic depth necessary and sufficient for graph connectivity, and that single-layer small-dimension transformers suffice for contextual retrieval. Transformers often beat specialised GNNs.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) Rethinking the Capacity of Graph Neural Networks for Branching Strategy</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-3939">link</a>]</summary> Defines the "MP-tractable" class of MILPs on which message-passing GNNs can approximate strong-branching scores, with a universal approximation theorem; outside it, impossible, and second-order GNNs are needed.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'23</i>) On Representing Mixed-Integer Linear Programs by Graph Neural Networks</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10759">link</a>]</summary> Finds "there exist feasible and infeasible MILPs that all GNNs will treat equally"; restores reliable prediction by restricting to unfoldable MILPs or adding random features.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'23</i>) On Representing Linear Programs by Graph Neural Networks</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.12288">link</a>]</summary> Constructs, for any size limit, a GNN separating distinct LPs; shows properly built GNNs reliably predict feasibility, boundedness and an optimal solution for a broad class. Proofs via the Weisfeiler–Lehman connection.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>AAAI'21</i>) Provably Good Solutions to the Knapsack Problem via Neural Networks of Bounded Size</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v35i9.16939">link</a>]</summary> ReLU RNNs applied iteratively over items compute optimal or provably good Knapsack values: depth four with width quadratic in the optimal profit for exact solutions; depth five, width w for a 1 − O(n²/√w) approximation, with matching lower bounds.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'20</i>) What graph neural networks cannot learn: depth vs width</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03199">link</a>]</summary> Message-passing GNNs are Turing universal under conditions on depth, width, node attributes and layer expressiveness, and lose much of that power when depth and width are restricted. Impossibility results imported from distributed computing.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'20</i>) What Can Neural Networks Reason About?</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13211">link</a>]</summary> Defines algorithmic alignment and derives a sample-complexity bound that decreases with better alignment; unifies intuitive physics, VQA and shortest paths through dynamic programming; shows GNNs align with DP.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'19</i>) Approximation Ratios of Graph Neural Networks for Combinatorial Problems</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10261">link</a>]</summary> Bridges GNN theory and distributed local algorithms; gives tight achievable-and-not-improvable approximation ratios for minimum dominating set, minimum vertex cover and maximum matching, and shows colouring features change them.</details>

### Learnability, Sample Complexity and Size Generalisation

Whether the representable thing can be found from data, and whether it survives a change of instance size.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICLR'26</i>) How hard is learning to cut? Trade-offs and sample complexity</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00252">link</a>]</summary> Sample-complexity <b>lower</b> bounds for cut selection, valid for both score functions used in the literature (tree size and gap closed); learning over an unknown instance distribution needs at least as many samples as learning a generic target from the same class. Nearly tight against known upper bounds.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>COLT'26</i>) Graph neural networks extrapolate out-of-distribution for shortest paths</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/nerem26a.html">link</a>]</summary> Proves a GNN minimising a sparsity-regularised loss over a small set of shortest-path instances implements K steps of Bellman-Ford, with ε-minimisation giving O(ε)-accurate distances.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'26</i>) Learning to Execute Graph Algorithms Exactly with Graph Neural Networks</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23207">link</a>]</summary> Proves exact learnability for graph algorithms under bounded-degree and finite-precision constraints: train an MLP ensemble on a single node's local instructions, then use it as the GNN update function at inference. NTK analysis; covers flooding, BFS, DFS, Bellman-Ford, and the LOCAL model.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'26</i>) Which Algorithms Can Graph Neural Networks Learn?</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13106">link</a>]</summary> A framework giving sufficient conditions for MPNNs to learn an algorithm from small instances and provably approximate it at arbitrary size with worst-case guarantees, covering SSSP, MST and DP problems including 0-1 knapsack; plus impossibility results and stronger architectures. Refines Bellman-Ford with substantially smaller training sets and a differentiable regularizer.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'25</i>) Generalization Guarantees for Learning Score-Based Branch-and-Cut Policies in Integer Programming</b> [<a href="https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ac04e54e0a2d1927d60709019e4e7870-Abstract-Conference.html">link</a>]</summary> Sample-complexity bounds for learned B&amp;C policies whose scoring functions are piecewise polynomial, covering node selection, cut selection and branching variable selection.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'25</i>) On Transferring Transferability: Towards a Theory for Size Generalization</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23599">link</a>]</summary> A framework for transferability across input sizes: transferability corresponds exactly to continuity in a limit space that identifies small instances with equivalent large ones, the identification determined by data and task. Instantiated on existing architectures with design principles.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'25</i>) Learning to Add, Multiply, and Execute Algorithmic Instructions Exactly with Neural Networks</b> [<a href="https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/71553eb7d97b9c332d9c520c5de724d9-Abstract-Conference.html">link</a>]</summary> NTK analysis of two-layer networks in the infinite-width limit shows a large enough ensemble can be trained to execute binary permutations, addition, multiplication and SBN instructions exactly with high probability, from logarithmically many training samples. SBN is Turing-complete.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'24</i>) Sample Complexity of Algorithm Selection Using Neural Networks and Its Applications to Branch-and-Cut</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-0789">link</a>]</summary> Extends data-driven algorithm design from picking one algorithm to picking one <i>per instance</i> with a neural network, with sample-complexity bounds, instantiated on branch-and-cut.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>NeurIPS'21</i>) Sample Complexity of Tree Search Configuration: Cutting Planes and Beyond</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04033">link</a>]</summary> Sample-complexity bounds for cut selection in branch-and-cut, for Chvátal–Gomory cuts and beyond, handling any number of waves of any number of cuts and tuned to constraint-coefficient magnitudes.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'18</i>) Learning to Branch</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/balcan18a.html">link</a>]</summary> Learns an optimal weighting over any set of variable-partitioning procedures for a given instance distribution, with sample-complexity guarantees.</details>

### Barriers and Impossibility

Results that close a door, and the exact conditions under which they do.

- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'26</i>) Theoretical Challenges in Learning for Branch-and-Cut</b> [<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23249">link</a>]</summary> Shows local score-based methods can produce search trees exponentially larger than optimal, from two sources: expert signals misaligned with tree size, and amplification of small discrepancies by the B&amp;B recursion.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>PNAS'23</i>) Barriers for the performance of graph neural networks (GNN) in discrete random structures</b> [<a href="https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2314092120">link</a>]</summary> Two theorems, not one, and they cover different problems. <b>Theorem 0.1</b> — independent set on the random d-regular graph <code>Gd(n)</code>: for any GNN architecture, any feature dimensions, any update functions and any number of iterations R, the set produced "is at most half-optimum asymptotically in d". The mechanism is that an R-iteration GNN is an R-local algorithm, and OGP bars all R-local algorithms; greedy already attains 1/2, so this "leaves very little space". <b>Theorem 0.2</b> — MaxCut on the random K-uniform hypergraph <code>G(n,d;K)</code>, <b>K ≥ 4 and even</b>: the cut is capped at the Branching-OGP threshold, which AMP already attains.</details>
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>ICML'20</i>) It’s Not What Machines Can Learn, It’s What We Cannot Teach</b> [<a href="https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/yehuda20a.html">link</a>]</summary> Proves that, assuming NP ≠ coNP, any polynomial-time sample generator for an NP-hard problem "samples, in fact, from an easier sub-problem"; shows empirically via Conjunctive Query Containment that standard generation practice biases datasets.</details>

## Selection Criteria

This is a deliberately curated collection, not a systematic literature search.

- **Every entry has been read** and its one-line summary written from the paper's own text. Depth of reading varies and is not uniform: most of the list was read at abstract depth, a minority at passage depth, and the theory entries whose claims this list characterises were read at theorem level. Where a summary states what a theorem requires, that came from the theorem.
- **Scope test.** A paper is in when a computational task a classical algorithm was previously performing is instead performed, wholly or in part, by a learned model. Generic graph representation learning is out. Work that embeds a classical solver and differentiates through it without learning anything about the solver is a neighbouring field, and is listed under [Adjacent Fields](#adjacent-fields) rather than surveyed.
- **Venue labels name the venue of record.** Where DBLP holds a formal publication, its label and its electronic edition — OpenReview, PMLR, conference proceedings, or a publisher DOI — are used. Otherwise the label comes from the corpus metadata and the link is the arXiv abstract page. Papers that exist only as preprints are labelled `arXiv`; workshop and satellite tracks are labelled as such.
- **Link text is `link`, not `pdf`** — each URL points at the paper's official page, which is not always a direct PDF. Where the page of record is behind a paywall and a free preprint exists, an `arXiv` link is added alongside. Two entries have no DOI, no arXiv record and no OpenReview page, and link to their Semantic Scholar record instead.
- **No PDFs are hosted here.** Open access is not the same as a licence to redistribute, and a fair number of these papers carry neither.
- **Preprints are included on their merits.** Several entries here are arXiv-only and are among the most important work in the field; venue is a signal, not the criterion.
- **Known gaps.** Coverage of pre-2015 classical work is thin by design. One paper in the physics-inspired GNN debate is missing and is named in that section.
- **No paper counts are used as evidence.** Section sizes reflect what this list happens to hold, not the shape of the field.

## Contributing

If a paper is missing, miscategorised, or its summary misstates what it claims, please open an issue or a pull request. Corrections to the [Debates](#debates-and-replications) and [Theory](#what-the-theory-actually-proves) sections are especially welcome — those make claims about what results do and do not cover, and a wrong one should be fixed.

Entries use a collapsible summary, so the one-liner stays hidden until the title is clicked:

```html
- <details style="display:inline"><summary><b>(<i>Venue'YY</i>) Paper title</b> [<a href="url">link</a>]</summary> One-line summary.</details>
```

The summary line is raw HTML, so use `<b>` / `<i>` / `<a>` rather than Markdown syntax inside it.
